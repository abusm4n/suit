
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Software updates are essential to smart home IoT security, yet the security of update delivery remains largely underexplored at the network level.
We presented a multidimensional analysis of IoT update traffic, combining entropy-based encryption characterization, cipher-suite evaluation, and certificate assessment across controlled experiments on ten devices and a large-scale retrospective dataset.
Update traffic still relies on TLS~1.2, with a substantial cleartext share on the update paths of privacy-sensitive devices.
On \emph{confidentiality}, entropy remains below the 0.80 threshold in both settings (0.78 controlled, 0.71 retrospective) and below the baseline reported for general encrypted IoT traffic, with complete firmware binaries recoverable from the update traffic for some devices.
On \emph{cipher suites}, insecure and weak suites dominate what clients offer (78.2\%), leaving devices dependent on correct server-side configuration for the protection they actually receive and without recourse if a server is misconfigured or compromised.
On \emph{authenticity and cryptographic strength}, certain IoT devices exposed no TLS certificate in their update traffic, and others relied at least partly on self-signed certificates outside the public CA trust chain, while certificates average 115.4-bit strength with 8.74-year validity, leaving a non-trivial share in service past the NIST 2031 transition.
Mapping these configurations to public vulnerability records yields 92 CVEs spanning broken or risky algorithms, inadequate encryption strength, and improper certificate validation, with impacts ranging from traffic interception and man-in-the-middle downgrade to brute-force key recovery and arbitrary code execution, and with severity trending upward over the past five years.
